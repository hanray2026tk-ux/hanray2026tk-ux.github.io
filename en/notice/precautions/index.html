Face Contouring · Precautions

Before and After Surgery Precautions

This checklist puts the things you actually have to do before and after facial contouring surgery in order. It does not replace your surgeon's assessment or your personal instructions, but it helps you avoid the usual detours during preparation and recovery.

Before SurgeryAfter Surgery

Before Surgery

BEFORE

From the decision to the operating room, the goal is to deal with controllable risks in advance.

2 weeks before · medication and habits

Stop aspirin, vitamin E, fish oil and supplements that affect bleeding. Stop smoking and keep alcohol to a minimum. Do not stop prescribed medication on your own — check with us first.

1 week before · general condition

Avoid strenuous exercise and facial massage. Tell us about a cold, fever, mouth ulcer, gum inflammation or skin infection on the face so the date can be adjusted if needed.

1 day before · fasting and hygiene

Fast as instructed (usually no food for 8 hours and no water for 4 hours). Wash your hair and face; no makeup, skincare or styling products.

1 day before · oral preparation

For an intraoral incision, have scaling and any cavities treated beforehand, and rinse with mouthwash the night before and on the morning of surgery.

Surgery day · what to bring and wear

Wear loose, front-opening clothing. Leave jewellery, contact lenses and nail polish off (they interfere with monitoring). Bring previous imaging and a list of your current medication.

Tell us in advance

High blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid conditions, bleeding disorders, drug allergies, and whether you are pregnant or breastfeeding.

After Surgery

AFTER

How well you care for yourself during recovery shapes both the speed of swelling resolution and how clean the final contour looks.

Cold compress and positioning

Use intermittent cold compresses for the first 72 hours, 15–20 minutes at a time with about an hour in between. Sleep with your head raised 15–30° to help venous return.

Incisions and oral care

With an intraoral incision, rinse with the prescribed mouthwash for 30 seconds after every meal. For one month, brush around the incision rather than over it and do not aim a water flosser at it. Keep small facial incisions dry and change dressings as scheduled.

Diet

For the first two weeks, take cool or lukewarm liquids and soft food. Avoid spicy food, alcohol, very hot food and anything that needs hard chewing, which loads the masseter.

Swelling and bruising

Swelling peaks around days 3–5, is clearly reduced by about two weeks, and settles over one to three months. The two sides rarely resolve at the same speed, so daily mirror comparisons are not useful.

Activity limits

For two weeks avoid bending over, lifting heavy objects and strenuous exercise. For one month avoid facial massage, saunas, swimming and long exposure to heat.

Medication

Take antibiotics and pain relief as prescribed and do not increase the dose. Avoid combining them with medication or supplements that affect clotting.

Follow-up and stitch removal

Attend your scheduled visits; stitches are usually removed 7–14 days after surgery. Follow-up reviews bone healing, soft-tissue adaptation and how wide you can open your mouth.

Contact us immediately if

You have a persistent high fever, heavy bleeding from an incision, sharply increasing pain, clear difficulty opening your mouth or swallowing, or progressive facial swelling. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Judging the result

Contour changes appear gradually as swelling resolves; assessing the final shape after three months is more reliable. The points above are general guidance — stopping medication, rinsing routines and follow-up timing follow your surgeon's assessment and written instructions.
