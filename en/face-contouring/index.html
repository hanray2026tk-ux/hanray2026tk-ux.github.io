Why some contouring procedures fall short

Do not let one decision become a lifelong regret.

A few things to know before facial contouring surgery

  • Scenario1Angle only, thickness ignored
    Common concern

    “I had surgery, but the front view barely changed.”

    Why it happens

    Only the jaw angle was treated, yet frontal width comes mostly from bone thickness, not angle alone.

  • Scenario2Lost proportion, narrower and narrower
    Common concern

    “My face looks longer and narrower instead.”

    Why it happens

    One standard osteotomy pattern destroyed the original proportions.

  • Scenario3Lost dimension, looks older
    Common concern

    “My cheekbones are smaller, but my face looks older.”

    Why it happens

    Grinding away the cheekbone's highest point flattens the face, so it reads as flat and older.

We do more than shave an angle.
We care about proportion — not simply reducing, but rebuilding.

Two-dimensional analysis of bone thickness and angle

If appearance comes from bone, we must look at thickness as well as angle.

Why does the front view stay the same after jaw-angle surgery?

Treating only the jaw angle changes the profile, while frontal width often stays unchanged.

Anatomical reason

Many people have a
medially curved jaw structure.
If only the jaw angle is treated,
the profile becomes more delicate,
but frontal width barely changes.

Our approach
  • Two-dimensional analysis of bone thickness and angle
  • Jaw angle treatment: refines the profile
  • Thickness reduction: narrows the front view at the same time
  • Then a custom design classified by chin shape

Custom chin-shaping plan

Proportion decides appearance, so the plan must be tailored, not templated.

A single straight cut easily destroys the original proportions.

Chins differ from person to person; a template cut throws proportions off and looks unnatural.

FAQ

QWhat tests are needed before facial contouring surgery?
AUsually facial X-rays or a CT scan, plus baseline blood work and an ECG. Your surgeon considers bone shape and skin elasticity to choose the most suitable technique.
QWhat should I discuss at consultation?
ABring front, profile and 45-degree photos and describe what concerns you most, such as wide cheekbones or a long chin. Your surgeon will base the design on that.
QDoes facial contouring surgery hurt?
AThe procedure is performed under anesthesia, so it is essentially painless. Swelling and soreness are possible for two or three days, and your surgeon will arrange pain relief and care.
QHow long is recovery?
AMost people resume daily activities in two or three days, with marked swelling easing in one to two weeks. Full bone stability usually takes a few months.
QWhat should I eat after surgery?
AStart with warm or cool soft foods; avoid spicy, very hot and chewy foods, and keep the mouth clean.
QWill facial contouring leave scars?
AIncisions are usually placed inside the mouth, at the sideburns or along the hairline, so visible scars are generally not apparent after healing.
QWill I need to wear a compression garment?
ASome patients wear an elastic head band or face mask briefly to help swelling settle and shape the result; your surgeon sets the duration by technique.
QAre follow-up visits needed?
AYes. Regular visits as scheduled let your surgeon check bone healing and swelling, and adjust care if needed.
